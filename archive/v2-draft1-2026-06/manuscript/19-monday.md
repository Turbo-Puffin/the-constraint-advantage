# Monday.

No recap. You were there.

Eighteen chapters of gates and fences and you could, if you wanted, turn this into a project — reread with a highlighter, build the spreadsheet, schedule the overhaul for a clean future date. That instinct is the old operating system asking for one last job. Decline it. This book doesn't do bonfires, and it doesn't do launches either. It does Monday.

So here's Monday. Three steps, one week, nothing dramatic.

**Pick one limit.** Not the best one. Not the complete set. One — and small (chapter 4 taught you small is the serious version). The phone sleeps in the kitchen. The Saturday gets a fence. The number gets set. One standing thing, deleted. You've known which one since chapter 1; it's the thing you've been carrying that you never chose. Put it down.

**Make it a rule, not a hope.** Say it in one sentence with no judgment calls left in it. Decide it once, in daylight, while you're strong. You know why this works now. That knowledge was most of the book.

**Tell the people it's for.** Not an announcement — God spare them the announcement — just the plain version, at the table: *"the phone's going to live in the kitchen at night now."* Out loud, because out loud is what keeps it from eroding, and because the watchers should see a rule get *chosen*. It's the best thing you can show them, and class, you'll recall, is always tomorrow.

Then hold it for a week, and pay attention to one thing only: not whether you're more productive, not whether you feel transformed — transformation is cabin-talk. Just watch **what wanders into the space.** Something always wanders in. It's usually short. It's usually been waiting.

That's the whole assignment. Next Monday, hang another gate if you want one. Or don't — sit in the quiet you bought and call it enough, because *enough exists*, and you now know how to locate it, and locating it was the success the whole time.

A constraint is just a promise with architecture. You've been making promises your whole life — at altars, in delivery rooms, in the truck on the way home from games, silently, to small sleeping people who never heard them.

This is just how you build the ones that hold.

It's not a system. It's how you live now.

Go home. You know the way — and for the first time in a long time, there's nothing else running.

*The front row is keeping score.*
*Give them something to write down.*
